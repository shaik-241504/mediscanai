🌡️

Fever Reduction & Clinical Uses

Acts directly on the temperature-regulating center of the brain (hypothalamus) to bring down high fever, and blocks pain messenger chemicals (prostaglandins) in the central nervous system.

Approved Indications:

  • ✓ High body temperature & viral/bacterial fever
  • ✓ Headache, tension migraine & toothache
  • ✓ Muscular soreness, body ache & back pain
  • ✓ Post-vaccination fever & joint stiffness
💧

How to Use

Take orally with a glass of water. Can be taken on an empty stomach or after meals. Maintain a minimum gap of at least 4 to 6 hours between doses. Do NOT crush or chew prolonged-release tablets.

⏰

When to Take

Take as needed (SOS/PRN) when body temperature exceeds 100°F (37.8°C) or upon onset of headache or body soreness. Allow 4-6 hours before considering another dose.

👥

Suitability & Dosage Guidelines by Age Group

👨‍🦰 Adults (18-64 Y) SAFE

500 mg - 650 mg every 4-6 hours

Max 4,000 mg (4g) in 24 hours.

Highly safe and first-line fever reducer for healthy adults. Never combine with other paracetamol products.

👶 Children (Pediatric) CAUTION

10-15 mg/kg per dose

Max 4 doses in 24 hours.

Do NOT give adult 650mg tablets! Use pediatrician-prescribed pediatric oral drops/syrup with calibrated measuring dropper.

👴 Senior Citizens (65+) ADJUSTED

500 mg every 6-8 hours

Max 2,000 - 3,000 mg in 24 hours.

Lower dosage ceiling recommended due to age-related decline in hepatic clearance and renal filtration. Monitor liver function.

⚖️

How Much Drug (Dosage Limit & Overdose Safety Meter)

Max Adult Limit: 4,000 mg / 24h
0 mg (Start) 1,300 mg (2 Tabs) 2,600 mg (4 Tabs) 4,000 mg (CRITICAL CEILING)
⚠️ OVERDOSE DANGER ALERT: Consuming more than 4,000 mg (4 grams) in 24 hours can deplete hepatic glutathione, causing acute liver necrosis / liver failure. Never combine with cough syrups containing paracetamol, and strictly avoid alcohol.

ℹ️ Common / Mild Problems

Mild nausea Mild dizziness Well-tolerated at normal doses

⚠️ Serious Problems & Severe Reactions

• Acute liver toxicity & jaundice (in overdose or chronic alcohol intake)

• Allergic swelling of face, lips, tongue, or throat (Angioedema)

• Stevens-Johnson syndrome (severe skin blistering & peeling - rare)

🚨

When to Contact a Doctor Immediately (Emergency Red Flags)

EMERGENCY ACTION
  • 1. High fever remains above 102°F (38.9°C) or lasts more than 3 consecutive days without reduction.
  • 2. Signs of severe allergic reaction: swelling of face, lips, tongue, or sudden difficulty breathing or wheezing.
  • 3. Signs of liver distress: yellowing of skin or whites of eyes (jaundice), tea-colored dark urine, or severe upper right stomach pain.
  • 4. Suspected accidental double-dose or intake exceeding 4,000 mg in 24 hours.
  • 5. Development of an unexplained red skin rash, hives, or peeling blisters.
